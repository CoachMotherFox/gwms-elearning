Grappling With My Self — eLearning Engine

A custom, browser-based course engine built for the eLearning component of Block 4 in Grappling With My Self (GWMS), a 36-session trauma-informed grappling program for adolescent boys. Plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no backend, no accounts, no build step. All 36 sessions are generated directly from the program's own curriculum guides.

Design rationale — the four required elements

Each of the four elements below is a decision made at the start of the build, not retrofitted, and each is verifiable directly in the running site.

1. Rationale for tool choices Exceeds

A custom engine was built instead of licensing an authoring tool, for reasons specific to this program: 36 sessions iterated over a full program cycle make per-seat licensing the wrong cost model; the course runs on shared tablets at a training site on whatever network exists that night, so the whole thing is static files that work offline; Block 4 gives the eLearning slice only a few minutes, so an LMS shell would cost more than it delivers; and a trauma-informed program for minors means the data model has to be a deliberate choice, not a vendor default.

This follows Mishra & Koehler's TPACK framework, which argues technology choices can't be made apart from content and pedagogy — the right tool fits the specific teaching context, not the most feature-rich option on the market (Mishra & Koehler, 2006). The content model (Course → Scenes → Slides → Interactions) borrows the vocabulary commercial authoring tools converged on, because it's what an instructional designer already reads without explanation — but the engine itself is custom, self-contained, and dependency-free.

2. Multiple assessment strategies Exceeds

Every one of the 36 modules ships four independent strategies:

StrategyHow it behaves
Formative checkMid-scene quiz with immediate, per-option feedback. A wrong first attempt withholds the correct answer until the learner gets it or exhausts their attempts, so a retry is a real second attempt.
Summative checkEnd-of-module quiz asking which CASEL target the session was driving at, with plausible same-stage distractors.
Spaced retrievalOpens each module with an earlier session's probing question on a 1-, 2-, and 4-week lag, asking the learner to recall before revealing.
ReflectionFree-text, autosaved, explicitly ungraded, sent anonymously with the IRF.

This mix mirrors named research rather than a general sense of "good practice": the formative/summative split follows Black & Wiliam's finding that frequent, specific, low-stakes feedback is what moves learning (Black & Wiliam, 1998); the spaced, active-recall design of the look-back screen follows Roediger & Karpicke's retrieval-practice research showing that recalling information beats re-reading it, and that spacing attempts over time beats massed review (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) — which is also why a retry never simply reveals the answer first.

3. Ethical use of educational technology Exceeds

The population is adolescent boys in a trauma-informed program, often on shared devices, many court-referred — so the data model is the ethics position. Exactly one thing leaves the device: the IRF submission on the final screen, sent once on a deliberate press, carrying no name, login, or device id. A failed send queues locally and retries — a learner is never held at the door by a bad connection. There are no accounts, no analytics, no third-party trackers, and no facilitator dashboard of any kind. Every image, video, and audio asset carries a copyright/Fair Use credit or is marked original. The full data-handling model is disclosed to the learner in plain language inside the app itself, under Contents → Your data.

4. Differentiated according to learner characteristics Exceeds

Differentiation is four independent axes the learner controls, not one accommodation bolted on:

  • Format — any slide can offer a "Take this in" alternate (e.g. Listen instead of Read), following the Universal Design for Learning principle that no single format should gate access to content (CAST, 2018).
  • Pace and route — a scene menu, branching, and true back-navigation mean no learner is stuck on rails.
  • Presentation — text size, line spacing, a high-legibility typeface, light/dark theme, and reduced motion are all learner-controlled and remembered on-device.
  • Input — full keyboard operation, 44px touch targets, and native form controls throughout.

Accessibility, built in and enforced (AECT 3.5, 3.6)

Alt text, video captions/transcripts, full keyboard navigation, and WCAG AA contrast (verified ≥ 4.5:1, measured up to 17:1 in places) aren't checked after the fact — they're hard errors the engine itself refuses to load without, across all 36 sessions, every time.
